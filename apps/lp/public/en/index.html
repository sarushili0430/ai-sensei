Why we are building this

Answers are free now.
Students still fail the exam.

Photo-search math apps are already in every student’s pocket. The cost of getting an answer has gone to zero — and yet the exam still goes badly. The reason is simple: reading a solution and feeling “I understand” is not the same state as being able to say the steps and the reasons in your own words. From the inside, those two feel identical.

You read it. It makes sense.

The solution is clear on the page, so you move on to the next problem.

Then you try to say it.

“Why the discriminant here?” — and nothing comes out.

That distance is the gap.

Handing out answers is a solved problem. We build the side that finds the gaps.

This is all you do

Photograph → get taught → teach it back → a gap remains

One loop needs one problem you are stuck on, and a few minutes.

1

Photograph

Take a picture of the problem, together with your notes. The notes matter — they show your senpai how far you already got.

2

Get taught

Your senpai works it out on the board. Equations, arithmetic and figures go on the board; the voice only asks: “Look at D here — it’s positive, right? So?”

3

Teach it back

“Now explain that back to me.” The answer is already on the board — and you still may not be able to say it. That is the whole point.

4

A gap remains

Wherever you stalled becomes a gap on your karte. What you taught back is yellow; where you stalled is pink.

Math is never read aloud as speech. A real tutor stops talking while they write, and so does this one — which is also why the lessons stay cheap enough to give away one a day.

The karte

No score. Just the gaps.

There is no accuracy rate and no percentile. After a lesson, exactly three things remain.

  • What you explained — highlighted in yellow, in your own words.
  • The gaps — where you stalled. Not points lost: places about to be filled.
  • Terminology — words you mixed up, like “quadratic formula” and “discriminant”.

The only two numbers in the app are the days you kept going and the gaps you filled. No leaderboards, no comparison with anyone else.

Four promises

Break any one of them and it is a different product.

1

Teach — then make them teach it back.

The senpai teaches on the board and asks for it back on the spot. Never teach-and-done.

2

Never show a score.

We count the days you kept going and the gaps you filled. No XP, no leagues, no accuracy rate.

3

Passing is never shameful.

“I can’t explain this yet” is always one tap away, and what you couldn’t say becomes something worth having: a gap.

4

Never nag.

Notifications and the paywall are written as your senpai’s judgement — no numbers, no orders, no guilt.

The idea underneath

People notice the holes in their own understanding at the moment they try to teach someone else — the self-explanation effect, and the protégé effect found in teachable-agent research. Kataru-te folds both into a single loop.

FAQ

The usual questions

Can I use it somewhere I can’t speak?

The review side, yes — it’s one text question that takes about ten seconds, so a train or a shared living room is fine. The lesson itself (being taught on the board, then teaching it back) needs your voice. Teaching it back is the product, so we did not swap it for typing.

Does it actually give you the answer?

Yes — fully, on the board. Then it asks you to explain it back. This is not an app that withholds the answer; it is an app that checks what happened after you got it.

Are there scores or leaderboards?

No. No accuracy rate, no percentile, no comparison with other people. Only the days you kept going and the gaps you filled.

What if I can’t explain it?

Tap “I can’t explain this yet”. That is not a failure — it is one gap found. It comes back tomorrow, in 3 days and in 7 days, and nothing scolds you in between.

Can the senpai teach something wrong?

It can. That is exactly why the loop does not end at teaching: when you teach it back, a misreading shows up as an explanation that does not hold together. Anything that felt wrong — an explanation, the board, a question — can be reported from Settings.

What happens to my photos and my voice?

The notebook photo and a transcript of the conversation are stored to build your karte, linked to an anonymous device ID — no name, no email address. There are no ads, and nothing is tracked across other apps or sites.

They are also used to improve the service: finding explanations that were unclear or wrong. On the free plan this is part of using the app; on Premium you can switch it off in settings. The full details are in the privacy policy （日本語）.

Any subject other than math?

Not yet. Before adding subjects we want one of them to prove that teaching it back really works.
